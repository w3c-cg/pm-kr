Procedural Texture Rendering

This 3D courtyard appears almost at once, even on a dial-up connection. Each surface is first drawn from a tiny recipe, called a procedure, and its photograph takes over when its download ends.

Pick a connection speed in the panel and press “Restart with these settings”.

A working demo by Daniel Ramos, offered as input to the W3C PM-KR Community Group, and a proposal for the glTF 3D format. It is not part of glTF yet.

The scene

About this demo

How the scene loads

  1. Every procedure arrives first. All seven together are about 15 KB.
  2. The scene is drawn at once from the procedures.
  3. The pictures (6.26 MB) download beside the drawing.
  4. Each procedure blends into its picture when the picture has arrived.
  5. That procedure then stops being computed.

What is simulated

The connection speed is simulated in this page. Your browser downloads each file at your own speed, and the page releases its bytes at the speed you choose, as Macromedia Flash's Simulate Download did. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Whose work this is

An individual contribution by Daniel Ramos, offered as input to the W3C Procedural Memory Knowledge Representation Community Group. It is not a report or a decision of the group.

The format

The scene is a glTF 2.0 file. Each texture names its procedure through a draft extension, KHR_texture_procedural_rendering, written as a proposal for glTF. A viewer that does not know it shows the pictures. Read the proposal

In 3D

Where the browser offers a WebXR device, “Enter 3D” draws one view for each eye. In an ordinary browser this is a 2D page, and the panel says so.
