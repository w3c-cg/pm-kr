Contrastive dual memory on WebGPU

A positive memory, a negative memory three times its size, and a decisor, as three compute dispatches per step. The task, the notation and the two metrics follow Dave Raggett's dual-memory demo (W3C Cognitive AI). The negative memory follows Daniel Campos Ramos's W- contrastive formulation, open and engine-agnostic. Everything runs on the GPU in your browser; nothing is installed and nothing leaves this page.

Results

Chance for every pattern position is 6.25%.

Acquisition curve: mean of p2 and p3 per exposure; the gap exposures carry no pattern.


  


  

    
What runs

    
Each step is three dispatches, in order. Dispatch 1 updates the state h = alpha h + (1 - alpha) E[s], normalizes it to the query q, and reads the positive memory: every stored (key, symbol) votes with weight exp(beta (key . q - 1)). Dispatch 2 reads the negative memory the same way, shaped by the chosen formulation (one pass; a recursive pass that first pulls q toward the stored negatives; a sign-flipped SiLU gate; a bounded -tanh; three parallel slabs averaged). Dispatch 3 is the decisor: it scores every symbol from the positive evidence and the negative evidence, predicts, measures surprise, and writes: the surprising truth into the positive memory, and the positive memory's best wrong alternative into the negative memory, until the negative memory separates the two by a margin. The metrics accumulate on the GPU and are read once at the end.
