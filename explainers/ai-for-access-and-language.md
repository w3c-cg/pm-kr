# AI for access and language: welcome by default

**Status:** an individual's input to the Procedural Memory Knowledge Representation Community Group, by Daniel Campos Ramos, 6 November 2026.
It is not a decision or a report of the group.
Comments and objections are welcome in this repository's issues.
**Why in PM-KR:** the charter wants humans and AI systems to consume the same source, and the people who reach that source through captions, speech or translation are the first test of whether it is reachable at all.

## The question

Some people can only speak through AI, some use AI to see, and some need speech turned into text to take part in a meeting.
Many more think in one language and write in another.
A policy on AI in standards work that is silent about them, or that asks them to announce their tools before every reply, shuts the door it meant to guard.
I think in Portuguese and prepare English with AI help; the ideas and the checking are mine.

## The idea

- **Accessibility tools are welcome by default.**
  Live captions, speech to text, description of what is on screen: a participant's tools, with their output staying with that participant unless the group agrees to share it ([AI policy input, point 2](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-ai-policy/README.md#2-accessibility-tools-should-be-welcome-by-default)).
  A human captioner cannot be in every room.
- **Language help is not "substance generated by AI".**
  Help with grammar, translation and formulating one's own thoughts should be named as such, so that no one has to disclose it before replying to a discussion ([point 3](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-ai-policy/README.md#3-a-person-may-need-ai-to-reply)).
  The person understands, approves and answers for the reply, with the support they need to make it.
- **Explain in your own language.**
  A participant may explain an idea in their language and work with an AI partner on the English draft; the draft keeps the meaning, the uncertainty and the attribution, and the author keeps editorial control.
  The repository's [editorial principles](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp/blob/main/docs/EDITORIAL_PRINCIPLES.md) say it in two languages: "Se for mais fácil para você, pode explicar em sua própria língua."
- **A judgement on style is not a judgement on substance.**
  When content is dismissed as appearing to fail a requirement, the contributor should be told which requirement and given a chance to fix it, because an appearance-based judgement falls first on people writing English with help ([point 13](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-ai-policy/README.md#13-give-a-reason-and-a-chance-to-correct)).

## Where it exists today

- The examples that show why: Jennifer Wexton's AI-assisted speech in the House, Ann Johnson's speech through a brain implant and AI, and Be My AI for blind and low-vision users, each linked from the [input page](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-ai-policy/README.md#2-accessibility-tools-should-be-welcome-by-default); W3C's own COGA task force held a [session on AI in its work](https://github.com/w3c/tpac2026-breakouts/issues/67).
- In this group: [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) invites explanations in one's preferred language, and the respec-mcp skills carry the same invitation into every document task.
