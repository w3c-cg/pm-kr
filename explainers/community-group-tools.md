# Tools for a small Community Group

**Status:** an individual's input to the Procedural Memory Knowledge Representation Community Group, by Daniel Campos Ramos, 6 November 2026.
It is not a decision or a report of the group.
Comments and objections are welcome in this repository's issues.

## The question

A Community Group with two volunteer chairs works on GitHub and a mailing list, meets rarely, and is judged by its activity and by how many independent parties contribute.
The tools it needs most are the ones that turn a question into a recorded decision and show a newcomer where to help.

## The idea

Four small things ([Community Group tools input](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-cg-redesign/README.md)):

1. **Activity where the group works.**
   The group page should show the issues and the list, not only the blog, so someone arriving finds the questions participants can help answer (point 5).
2. **Decisions recorded in the meeting.**
   A simple voting tool for chairs and members, public when the group chooses, connected to the IRC channel and the call, so a question asked in a meeting becomes a decision in the minutes (point 7).
   At the redesign session the room established that W3C's questionnaire tool serves only closed groups today, which records the gap ([minutes](https://www.w3.org/2026/10/28-cg-program-minutes.html)).
3. **Consideration as well as a count.**
   The draft transfer considerations ask whether multiple independent parties contribute; the assessment could also show whether newcomers' contributions received an answer and consideration (point 8).
4. **A stepping stone in three lines.**
   Ask every participant: what exists today, with a link; the next increment, and the value it has on its own; what it needs from others.
   Collect the answers on one page ([Stepping stones I can show](../talks/2026-11-03-stepping-stones/README.md#a-question-for-every-participant)).
