# What it costs to follow every clause

**Status:** an individual's input to the Procedural Memory Knowledge Representation Community Group, by Daniel Campos Ramos, 6 November 2026.
It is not a decision or a report of the group.
Comments and objections are welcome in this repository's issues.
**Why in PM-KR:** a representation that follows every clause of a specification depends on reading that clause, so the price of the text is a cost of the knowledge itself, and a free twin is the difference between a rule that can be checked and one that cannot.

## The question

A stereo file read with half of its specification is read wrongly for someone: the eye order, the packing and the track association are each one clause, and without the clause the code guesses, which shows up on a screen as swapped eyes or a missing view.
So the work follows every clause, and a rule that cannot be tied to a clause is marked as a reading and kept apart.
What does the text of every clause cost the person doing that work?

## The numbers

Prices checked on 4 November 2026 on each issuer's page, for one user and an electronic copy ([the full table](../talks/2026-11-04-paid-standards/README.md)):

- the two texts the Android work needs today, ISO/IEC 23000-11:2009 and ISO/IEC 14496-15:2024, cost CHF 362, which is 1,49 Brazilian monthly minimum wages, or 44,8 days of work at that wage;
- everything in the table, amendments included, adds up to CHF 1.491 and USD 479, 7,78 minimum wages or 233 days, and that is a floor: HDMI, Miracast, DisplayPort, SMPTE, Blu-ray and the IEC audio packs publish no price or sell only to members;
- HDMI goes only to adopters paying every year, the 3D part of HDMI 1.4a excepted, which anyone may read but only "for the purposes of informing itself ... and/or evaluating" it;
- a registered engineer's access to Brazilian standards leaves out exactly ISO and IEC.

The same knowledge is open elsewhere, and the people who keep it open deserve thanks by name: the ITU-T publishes H.264, H.265, H.273 and H.274 as free downloads; ISO and IEC list ISO/IEC 14496-12:2026, where the StereoVideoBox lives, at no charge; ETSI publishes the DVB 3DTV specification; CIPA publishes the MPO and stereo still image formats; the IETF publishes RFC 9559; the Web3D Consortium, W3C and Khronos publish theirs in the open.

## The idea

Standards bodies explain that selling their texts pays for developing and maintaining them, and this input does not dispute that.
It puts the cost next to the work that has to follow every clause, and next to the people who would do that work.
Two things follow for a group like this one:

- cite the free twin wherever one exists (ITU-T H.264 for ISO/IEC 14496-10, H.273 for 23091-2), and say "not read here" where only a paid text defines a formula ([the glossary's where-the-texts-come-from](../glossary/README.md#where-the-texts-come-from));
- the member companies in the room can carry a question into HDMI and Miracast where an individual cannot ([the list, 30 October 2026](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-pm-kr/2026Oct/0021.html)).

## Questions for the group

1. Which paid text does your work depend on, and does it have a free twin?
2. Would your organisation carry a stereo question into a body it belongs to?
