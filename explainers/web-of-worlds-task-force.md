# Where the work belongs: a Web of Worlds Task Force

**Status:** an individual's input to the Procedural Memory Knowledge Representation Community Group, by Daniel Campos Ramos, 6 November 2026.
It is not a decision or a report of the group.
Comments and objections are welcome in this repository's issues.
**Why in PM-KR:** the charter asks how the group's knowledge model relates to other W3C technologies, and a map of where each piece of a Web of Worlds belongs is that relationship written down, with the group's own row in it.

## The question

A Web of Worlds, many addressable, linked spatial experiences, touches linking and addressing, composition and semantics, presentation on every screen, identity, assets, discovery, sound, media that carries stereo and depth, and testing.
No one group holds it, inside W3C or outside.
Where does the work belong, and how does it get done rather than mapped?

## The idea

Everywhere a world runs: inside the browser and outside it, down to the operating system and the screens people own, not only headsets.
So: a joint Web of Worlds Task Force, hosted by the Community Groups that hold the pieces, with liaisons to the Working Groups and to the bodies outside W3C.
It maps each gap to the group where it belongs, builds a working prototype and a test for it, and hands it over when that group takes it ([Web of Worlds input, point 8](../talks/2026-10-30-tpac-web-of-worlds/README.md#8-where-the-work-belongs-a-web-of-worlds-task-force)).

The form exists: the W3C Process lets a chair form task forces within a group's charter (section 3.4.1); COGA is one task force serving two Working Groups; the Credentials Community Group's VC-EDU task force ran plugfests with implementers; spatial data has been joint work of W3C and OGC since 2015.
The map, one row per area with where to start it, the W3C groups that hold it and the bodies outside W3C, is in the glossary with every group's page ([the group map](../glossary/README.md#where-the-work-belongs-the-group-map)).

**Who else belongs on it.**
The Web3D Consortium already standardises what a world needs, X3D 4.0 (ISO/IEC 19775-1:2023) with its geospatial component and HAnim (ISO/IEC 19774-1:2019) for humanoids, and takes part in the Forum's Web of Worlds work; Knowledge3D's avatar specification uses both and is offered for Web3D's review ([spatial browsing input, point 7](../talks/2026-10-28-tpac-spatial-browser/README.md#7-add-the-web3d-consortium-to-the-organizations); [Web of Worlds input, point 5](../talks/2026-10-30-tpac-web-of-worlds/README.md#5-common-ground-with-web3d)).
The W3C Metaverse Interoperability Community Group (OMI), at work on glTF extensions for worlds since 2021, belongs on the map too.

**Each of us can act before any group exists.**
Aaron Swartz wrote RFC 3870 in 2004, the IANA registration of `application/rdf+xml`; in October 2026 IANA registered Matroska's depth planes and StereoMode 15 to 20 at an individual's request.
