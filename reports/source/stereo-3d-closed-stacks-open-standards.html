The first report, Stereo 3D on Linux and the Web: Established Specifications, Adopted in Pieces (October 2026), listed the stereo specifications that exist and how each attempt to adopt them stopped. This second report continues it after TPAC 2026. It puts two markets side by side with their sources: headset VR, contracting at every maker and studio in 2025 and 2026, and 3D television, which the makers left between 2016 and 2017. In both, the costly closed hardware went away while the open standards stayed: HDMI® 1.4a 3D is still on every 3D television, Blu-ray 3D titles still ship, and Matroska's stereo field is in an RFC.

The thesis is Daniel Campos Ramos's: closed stacks are the culprit, so the answer is to standardise and open, which is what the Sparky Stereo OS work does. The report shows it case by case, states the other causes (cost, content, comfort) and the counter-signals, separates the measured figures from the author's reasoning, and lists what has been opened since the first report: kernel, driver, compositor and player patches, two IANA registrations, two petitions to standards bodies, and a phone from 2019 serving as a headset. It ends with three questions a reader can act on.

This is an individual report by Daniel Campos Ramos, offered as input to the PM-KR Community Group for its consideration. It is not a W3C document, not a Community Group Report, and not a decision of the PM-KR Community Group, which Daniel co-chairs; the first report was offered to that group as an individual's input, and this one continues it in the same spirit. Sparky Stereo OS is in development: a developer ISO for testers exists, and the stereo edition ISO is still being prepared (status on the front page).

Discussion takes place on the group's public list, public-pm-kr@w3.org, and in the group's repository.

20 and 26 October: two IANA registrations

The IANA Matroska Track Plane Types registry assigned values 3, 4 and 5 to the depth maps of the left-eye, right-eye and background planes on 20 October 2026 (First Come First Served). On 26 October 2026 the Matroska Stereo Modes registry assigned values 15 through 20 to packed picture-and-depth layouts after designated-expert review (Specification Required). Daniel Ramos is the change controller for both; the reference is the immutable stereo-layer v1.9.0 specification. A registry assignment names a layout; whether a muxer, decoder or player supports it is a separate fact, checked per program.

W3C's TPAC 2026 took place in Dublin and online from 26 to 30 October (event page). Daniel took part remotely in the public breakout sessions. Three of their draft minutes carry the stereo points.

  • 30 October, "Unleashing a Spatial Web: Mapping W3C Standards to Support a Web of Worlds" (minutes, IRC log). Daniel, in the log: "Most people have current 2D and 3D devices, not headsets. The inline-stereo line refers to WebXR pull request #1436, merged by Rik Cabanier on 9 June 2026: a page may now draw both eyes outside an immersive session. That is the web platform's own step towards stereo on screens that are not headsets, and the first report's proposals build on it.

27 October: two petitions to standards bodies

A sink that declares 3D under HDMI® 1.4a must accept the mandatory set of 3D formats: frame packing and top-and-bottom at 1080p24, 720p50 and 720p60, and side-by-side half at 1080i50 and 1080i60. The Linux kernel's open implementation lists exactly that set in drm_edid.c (stereo_mandatory_modes). A cheap headset built from one phone-class panel split between two eyes cannot accept frame packing at 1920 by 2205 pixels, so it cannot declare 3D compliantly, and today such devices take side by side as plain 2D and skip the 3D signalling.

On 27 October 2026 Daniel wrote to HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc., with the HDMI Forum in copy, asking for a side-by-side-only sink profile at the panel's native timing and a low-cost licence and certification path for such headsets. The same day he wrote to the Wi-Fi Alliance, because wireless display belongs to Miracast, whose Wi-Fi Display specification already has an optional 3D video formats capability that the public overview does not mention. Each letter named the other body. The Wi-Fi Alliance answered that it has no current plans for such a profile. HDMI Licensing Administrator answered too, and Daniel's follow-up of 2 November made the request precise: it changes no specification, and asks for a reduced certification of the side-by-side half subset that HDMI® 1.4a already defines, a compliance and licensing matter. On 30 October he asked the member companies of both bodies, through W3C's Media and Entertainment Interest Group, to carry the two requests (public archive). The bodies' own words are correspondence and are not reproduced here.

The pricing point raised with them is Daniel's: a membership or certification fee that is the same number of dollars everywhere weighs very differently per country. W3C has scaled its membership fees by the World Bank's country income classification since 2005 (W3C fees page); that precedent exists and is the one offered. Theoretically, many certified makers paying less can outnumber a few paying more, and for a body paid per certified unit that means more income, not less; this is an argument to measure, not a measured fact.

Why: closed stacks, case by case

The first report found that nobody had to act: a decoder that ignores the frame packing message is conformant, and each step after that was reasonable on its own. This report adds the other half of the explanation. Where the stereo path ran through a closed stack, the path died with its owner's business decision; where it ran through an open standard, the path is still there, and the Sparky Stereo OS work could pick it up in 2026 with the same files, cables and televisions.

Five closed stacks

NVIDIA 3D Vision (2008 to 2019)
Stereo on PC needed NVIDIA's GPU, NVIDIA's driver and NVIDIA's glasses and emitter. When NVIDIA ended it with Release 418, every kit became a shelf object, and the games, Windows and monitors did not change. The 3D television path NVIDIA sold as 3DTV Play was HDMI® 1.4a signalling, a public standard; that part is what the edition's kernel work now provides on every GPU, with open drivers.
Apple Vision Pro (2024 to 2025)
One device at US$3,499 (Apple, January 2024), one operating system, one store. Shipments went from about 390,000 to about 45,000 units a year; production was reported halted and advertising cut. The stereo content made for it (Apple's spatial video and photos) is tied to Apple's formats and viewers; the first report's depth-photo section shows the edition reading those files on any screen, which Apple's platform does not offer.
Meta Quest and Horizon (2019 to 2026)
Every Quest app ships through Meta's store; App Lab was folded into it in August 2024 (Meta developer blog). Reality Labs lost US$19.2 billion in 2025 on US$2.2 billion of revenue, closed three studios and cut a tenth of its staff in January 2026, and studios funded for Horizon Worlds cut staff when that funding changed. When the owner of the store and the platform changes course, the whole ecosystem above it contracts at once; the rows above show it happening within two months.
Sony PlayStation VR2 (2023 to 2025)
Tied to one console at launch, it paused production in 2024 and lost first-party support. The two moves that lifted it were openings: a PC adapter that let it run SteamVR (with features lost on the way, because the link is proprietary), and price cuts, after which it sold.
HDMI® 2.1 (2017 to 2024)
The HDMI Forum restricted public access to its specification in 2021, and in February 2024 Alex Deucher (AMD) wrote on the kernel's bug tracker (drm/amd issue 1417) that the Forum had rejected AMD's proposal for open-source HDMI® 2.1 support, so Linux users with AMD GPUs cannot drive 4K at 120 Hz over HDMI® with the open driver (Phoronix, Tom's Hardware; press, the tracker page sits behind a bot check). The 3D signalling is not in that closed part: it is in HDMI® 1.4a, whose text the kernel implements in the open, which is why a petition for a cheap headset profile is addressed to the licensing body and not to a closed specification.

What stayed open survived

  • HDMI® 1.4a's 3D structures, vendor InfoFrame and EDID bits are on every 3D television sold from 2010 and in the Linux kernel; the edition's kernel series uses them on AMD, NVIDIA and Intel GPUs (how it works).
  • Blu-ray 3D's MVC is H.264's multiview extension (Annex G in H.264 V16 of 06/2026, Annex H in the 2009 edition); the discs play in 2D on every player, titles kept coming after the television makers left, and FFmpeg's decoders read the second view today.
  • Matroska's StereoMode is in RFC 9559 (2024, Steve Lhomme, Moritz Bunkus and Dave Rice), with an IANA registry that was extended in October 2026 by the registrations above.
  • H.264's and H.265's frame packing message is in the streams themselves, which is what the merged encoder, remuxer, server and player changes read and write.

Theoretically, a standard outlives a product because anyone may implement it after the vendor leaves; in practice, that is what happened to every item in this list and to none of the closed stacks above.

The other causes, stated plainly

Closed stacks are not the only cause, and this report does not claim they are.

  • Cost. The Vision Pro at US$3,499, the Steam Frame and the Quest 3 at prices above a console, and before them 3D televisions and shutter glasses sold at a premium.
  • Content. The first report recorded the BBC's and ESPN's exits in 2013; the rows above record studios closing when their one platform's funding changed.
  • Comfort. The literature is clear on where discomfort comes from: motion. Solimini's 2013 prospective study of 497 adults found 54.8% reporting symptoms after a 3D film against 14.1% after 2D (PLoS ONE); Read and Bohr's 2014 study of 433 viewers found about 14% with headache or eyestrain from stereo itself and about 8% reporting symptoms from the glasses or from expecting them (Ergonomics); Hoffman and colleagues (2008, Journal of Vision) and Shibata and colleagues (2011, Journal of Vision) measured the vergence-accommodation conflict and the zone of comfort it leaves on desktop displays. The edition's answer is its design, not a warning: 2D content stays at the screen plane with zero disparity, depth is drawn only inside stereo windows and kept within that zone, a desktop has no camera motion, and 2D is always one switch away (depth on the desktop).

Price against comfort: Daniel's argument

This section is the author's reasoning, and it says which parts rest on measurement.

The argument: paying a high price and receiving discomfort is part of the blame. Had people paid less, the same discomfort would have been expected and more tolerable, because people weigh cost against benefit. A US$3,499 headset that gives a headache after an hour is returned; a US$15 cardboard viewer that does the same is put back in the drawer and taken out again on the next rainy day.

What is measured:

  • The returns of the Vision Pro in its first two weeks were reported with comfort, headaches and weight as the leading reasons (The Verge, 14 February 2024; press, read through its coverage elsewhere; no return rate was published).
  • The PlayStation VR2 sold when its price fell (the July 2024 row), and Sony made the cut permanent in March 2025.
  • More than 15 million Cardboard viewers shipped at a price near US$15, and Google reported that their use declined over time while opening the SDK so the design could continue; a cardboard viewer with a 2019 phone is the device the edition's Flat Headset runs on today.
  • The general mechanism is in the consumer-satisfaction literature: Richard L. Oliver's expectation-disconfirmation model (1980, Journal of Marketing Research) expresses satisfaction as a function of prior expectation and of how far the experience departs from it.

What is reasoning: that price sets the expectation in Oliver's sense, so the same discomfort disconfirms more at a higher price; and that this, rather than the discomfort alone, explains why the costly closed devices were returned or shelved while cheap and open ones were kept. No study known to the author measures return rates of headsets against price and comfort together; that is a measurement a reader could make.

What we are opening

Sparky Stereo OS is in development; nothing below is a released product, and each link leads to the public record of the piece.

  • Kernel and drivers. HDMI® 1.4a 3D on amdgpu and NVIDIA's open kernel modules, the whole set of 3D structures, DisplayPort frame-sequential stereo behind a per-connector opt-in, a virtual display (VKMS) that speaks HDMI® 3D for testing without a 3D television, and deep colour (10, 12 and 16 bits per channel): the kernel series, NVIDIA #1386 (in review), testing without a 3D TV.
  • Mesa and the compositor. Mesa packs OpenGL, EGL and Vulkan stereo views and declares them to KWin; Stereo KWin takes one format from every program, full side by side declared once, and turns it into each screen's format at the output (compositor design, labour division).
  • Encoders, containers, servers and players. The 23 merged changes above, in the projects' own trees, so the stereo mark travels with the file from encoder to screen (the one list, programs in detail).
  • Registrations. The IANA Matroska plane types 3 to 5 and stereo modes 15 to 20, with their open specification in stereo-layer.
  • Petitions. The HDMI® and Wi-Fi Alliance letters of 27 October 2026 for a low-cost, certifiable 3D profile for single-panel headsets.
  • The web. WebCodecs #970, Media Capabilities #249, the SVGStereo proposal at the WICG, and the first report's five proposals for a browser.
  • Phones as headsets. Flat Headset and FreeRDP #14003, so a phone from 2016 onwards in a cardboard viewer is a stereo screen for the desktop, drawn by the PC.

Every piece is under a free licence in a public repository, which is the whole point of the thesis: when this author leaves, the path stays.
