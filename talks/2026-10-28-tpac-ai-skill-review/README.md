# AI skills and human review: six points from our practice

I co-chair the PM-KR Community Group with Milton Ponson, and I build Sparky Stereo OS with AI partners whose work I direct and check.
These are the points I would raise in the session, with their sources, in case the time runs out.
I welcome Simone Onofri and Marcos Cáceres's experiment with portable skills that help people review standards, including during design.

## 1. Existing skills to compare

I can offer the [public skills we use to maintain Sparky Stereo OS](https://github.com/Sparky-OS/sparky-stereo-os/tree/main/skills).
They ask people and AI partners for the same sources, checks and explicit limits.
Our [AI partner instructions](https://github.com/Sparky-OS/sparky-stereo-os/blob/main/skills/ai-partners/SKILL.md) ask for bounded tasks, checks against expected results and a record of what remains unverified.
I would be glad to compare them with your review workflow and learn where the demonstrated skills are published.

## 2. Evidence a person can check

[Marcos's review of respec-mcp](https://github.com/speced/respec/pull/5168) gave me concrete security and packaging problems to address.
It led to standalone incubation; the ReSpec pull request was closed without merging, and broader audits remain a later step.
I want a skill's finding to be just as easy for a person to check.
Could each finding include its source, a way to test it and what remains uncertain?
Several models agreeing would still leave me needing that evidence.

## 3. Shared vocabulary and rules

In [respec-mcp](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp), I keep the group's editorial rules in its repository so people and assistants can use the same checks.
Our [document-skill catalog](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp/blob/main/docs/W3C_SKILLS.md) routes writing and review tasks to guidance and tools; these are our project-maintained instructions.
I would like the same approach for defined terms, with their sources and dates kept beside them.
Could we connect review skills to the vocabulary guidance editors use?
Passing structural checks does not establish security, accessibility, translation accuracy, technical truth or group consensus.

## 4. Roles in the record, responsibility with me

I record which AI partners investigated, drafted or checked, using CRediT roles in our [provenance page](https://github.com/Sparky-OS/sparky-stereo-os/blob/main/docs/ai-provenance.md).
Applying those roles to tools is our practice.
I call this human-like provenance, not human-like status.
I still review, decide and answer for the contribution.

## 5. Language and accessibility in review

I think in Portuguese and use AI help to prepare English while keeping the ideas and checking mine.
Our [editorial principles](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp/blob/main/docs/EDITORIAL_PRINCIPLES.md) let authors explain ideas in their own language and retain authority over the English draft.
Could the review workflow welcome the author's language and accessibility support, then check that the draft preserves their meaning and uncertainty?
I want that check to remain part of human review.

## 6. A shared rule is a small working example

I designed [Knowledge3D](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/Knowledge3D) around knowledge that people and AI can share as procedures.
Its specifications are a design that the current code does not yet implement.
I built [Sparky Stereo OS](https://github.com/Sparky-OS/sparky-stereo-os) as its desktop foundation for current 2D and 3D devices, and I bring the experience to standards discussions as input from an individual.
I see a shared, inspectable review rule as a small practical example of the question the [PM-KR Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/pm-kr/) studies.
