# An open source program: six points from a small tool

I co-chair the PM-KR Community Group with Milton Ponson, and respec-mcp grew from the checks we needed while preparing reports.
These are the points I would raise in the session, with their sources, in case the time runs out.
I thank Ian Jacobs, François Daoust and Benjamin Young for inviting people who have a project to bring to the program.

## 1. A concrete candidate

I can bring [respec-mcp](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp), our tool for checking standards documents against rules maintained by each group.
We have offered to donate it.
I would welcome considering it through the program's pilot process.
It is still incubating, and I am bringing it as a candidate for consideration.

## 2. The next step for a small group

I have read the [pilot preparation guide](https://github.com/w3c-oss/open-source-program/blob/main/guide/pilot-preparation.md), and I understand from the [CG charter](https://github.com/w3c-oss/open-source-program/blob/main/cg/charter.md) that this Community Group develops the program's governance and does not select pilots.
Our tool grew from a small group's report work.
What evidence should I bring to the staff handling intake so they can assess respec-mcp?
The guide covers purpose, code owners, governance, licensing, a one-year roadmap and resources, and I would value a proportionate route through those questions for a small tool.

## 3. Clear ownership after a donation

I want a donation to leave clear responsibilities for releases, security reports and maintenance.
The [program repository](https://github.com/w3c-oss/open-source-program) lists Can I VC and WebMCP Polyfill as pilots whose experience we can learn from.
What minimum commitments should a small project's code owners and W3C agree before it becomes a pilot?
I read the [preparation guide](https://github.com/w3c-oss/open-source-program/blob/main/guide/pilot-preparation.md) as the start of that discussion, with capacity and resources still to be agreed.

## 4. Review without an AI account

In [respec-mcp](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/respec-mcp), I can share configured checks and their results through GitHub or a local command.
A reviewer can inspect them without an AI account.
I would like that access to be part of how we assess tools for small groups.
As [PM-KR's README](https://github.com/w3c-cg/pm-kr) explains, a passing result covers the configured checks; it does not validate consensus or technical claims.

## 5. Experience before transfer

[Marcos Cáceres's review of ReSpec #5168](https://github.com/speced/respec/pull/5168) gave me concrete security and packaging concerns to address and helped me move respec-mcp into its own repository.
The pull request was closed without merging, and he asked for experience from real use before possible transfer and broader audits.
How should I present that usage and the remaining review work to this program?
I see that review as a starting point for the discussion of maturity and responsibility.

## 6. Why this tool exists

I designed [Knowledge3D](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/Knowledge3D) so people and AI can share knowledge stored as procedures.
Its specifications describe the design; the current code does not yet implement them.
I built [Sparky Stereo OS](https://github.com/Sparky-OS/sparky-stereo-os) as the desktop foundation for current 2D and 3D devices.
I bring that experience to standards discussions as input from an individual, and respec-mcp helps me prepare it for human review in the [PM-KR Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/pm-kr/).
