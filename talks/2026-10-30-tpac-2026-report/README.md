# TPAC 2026: seven breakouts, one thread

I co-chair the PM-KR Community Group with Milton Ponson.
I joined seven breakouts remotely from Brasília and brought written input to each one, so that every point had its sources even when the time ran out.
The thread through all of them: knowledge, media and worlds should reach people on the devices they already own, with a provenance that people and AI can check.

## What I brought

- **Mind the gaps:** files in H.264, Matroska and MP4 already mark their stereo layout, and every browser drops it, so a 3D video plays as two flat halves. I proposed a six-step path that keeps today's behaviour at every step.
- **An AI policy for W3C:** sixteen points. Among them: record the tools' part without giving them standing; accessibility tools are welcome by default; one simple disclosure form across W3C.
- **The open source program:** respec-mcp, our tool for checking standards documents against rules each group maintains, offered as a candidate for the program. I also asked that W3C's vocabulary reach Community Group work, for provenance and connections.
- **Spatial browsing:** places that have no location; federated WebXR inside Sneeze; the same content on the screens people own; procedures first and the image last.
- **AI skills and human review:** the public skills we use to maintain Sparky Stereo OS, offered for comparison. They ask people and AI partners for the same sources, checks and explicit limits.
- **Community Group tools:** ten points. Among them: a label for individual input, and polls connected to the IRC channel and the call, so a question asked in a meeting becomes a decision in the minutes.
- **A Web of Worlds:** eight points for the knowledge layer. They range from semantic roles and addresses inside a world to presenting worlds on the screens people own, and they end with where the work belongs.

## Where the work belongs: the next step

In the last session I proposed a Web of Worlds Task Force that works inside and outside W3C.
It does not stop at the browser: it reaches the operating system and the screens people own, not only headsets.
The [minutes](https://www.w3.org/2026/10/30-spatial-web-msf-minutes.html) record the room's next step: a two-day workshop co-organised by W3C, the Metaverse Standards Forum and Khronos, starting with a program committee on its scope.
The workshop would explore use cases, principles, design trade-offs, the focus of a Community Group charter, and the relationships with groups inside and outside W3C.
[Point 8 of my input](../2026-10-30-tpac-web-of-worlds/README.md#8-where-the-work-belongs-a-web-of-worlds-task-force) maps where each area could start, the W3C groups that work on it and the bodies outside W3C.
It is ready as input for that scope.
The session's follow-up to the Web of Worlds group of the Metaverse Standards Forum listed that input as an "in-depth analysis", beside the session's slides and minutes.

## One step already taken

In October 2026, IANA registered Matroska's depth planes and its picture-plus-depth layouts at my request ([registry](https://www.iana.org/assignments/matroska), 20 and 26 October 2026).
It is the kind of step each participant can take before any group exists.
