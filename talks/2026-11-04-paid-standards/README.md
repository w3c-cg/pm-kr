# What it costs to follow every clause

Prices checked on 2026-11-04 on each issuer's own page, converted with the Banco Central do Brasil PTAX selling rate of 2026-11-03.
Amounts in reais use the Brazilian format.

## The work

Sparky Stereo OS carries stereoscopic 3D from the file to the screen: H.264, H.265, Matroska, MP4, HEIF, MPO, HDMI and EDID, and now Android.
One rule binds the work: every packing a specification lists, never a subset, and every rule tied to its clause.
A rule that cannot be tied to a clause is marked as our reading and kept apart.

## Why it must follow every clause

A stereo file read with half of its specification is read wrongly for someone.
The eye order, the packing and the track association are each one clause.
Without the clause the code guesses, and a guess shows up on a screen as swapped eyes or a missing view.
So the price of the text is a cost of the work itself.

## The totals

The two texts the Android work needs today are ISO/IEC 23000-11:2009 and ISO/IEC 14496-15:2024: CHF 362, which is R$ 2.423,12.
That is 1,49 monthly minimum wages, or 44,8 days of work at the minimum wage.
With the three amendments that change those texts: CHF 416, R$ 2.784,58, 1,72 minimum wages, 51,5 days.

Everything in the table, amendments included, adds up to CHF 1.491 and USD 479: R$ 12.611,69.
That is 7,78 monthly minimum wages, or 233 days of work.
It is a floor: HDMI, Miracast, DisplayPort, SMPTE, Blu-ray and the IEC audio packs are not in it.

The minimum wage is R$ 1.621,00 a month and R$ 54,04 a day ([Decreto 12.797](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/decreto/d12797.htm), 23 December 2025).

## HDMI and Miracast

HDMI carries the 3D picture from the computer to the television, and Miracast carries it over Wi-Fi.
Neither specification can be bought as a document.

- **HDMI goes only to adopters,** who sign an agreement and pay every year. The [HDMI adopter page](https://www.hdmi.org/adopter/index) does not publish the amounts. Reported terms: USD 10.000 a year above 10.000 units, or USD 5.000 a year plus USD 1 per unit below that, and a royalty of USD 0,15 per device (USD 0,05 with the HDMI logo, USD 0,04 with HDCP as well) ([Symmetry Electronics](https://symmetryelectronics.com/blog/what-are-the-licensing-costs-associated-with-hdmi), [HDMI Licensing, 2006](https://design-reuse.com/news/13854/hdmi-annual-fee-reduced-hdmi-growth-fee-reduction-making-hdmi-even-more-affordable-all.html)).
- **In reais,** the smallest adopter fee is R$ 27.467,50 a year, 16,94 minimum wages, and the larger one is R$ 54.935,00 a year, 33,89 minimum wages, before any royalty.
- **The HDMI Forum,** which writes HDMI 2.1 and later, charges its members USD 12.500 a year, R$ 68.668,75, 42,36 minimum wages ([FAQ](https://hdmiforum.org/about/faq)).
- **The 3D part of HDMI 1.4a is the one exception.** HDMI Licensing sends it to anyone who gives an email address, but its licence allows using it only "for the purposes of (a) informing itself of such 3D video format and signaling and/or (b) evaluating" it ([hdmi.org](https://www.hdmi.org/docs/hdmi14bspecs)). It can be read, not built with.
- **Miracast** is the Wi-Fi Alliance's Wi-Fi Display specification, which the Alliance owns ([Wi-Fi Alliance](https://www.wi-fi.org/node/6909)). Developers report it can be downloaded after registering and accepting a licence that does not allow sharing it. Miracast certification requires Alliance membership, and the [membership page](https://www.wi-fi.org/membership) does not publish its dues.

## The same knowledge, open

The same technical text can be open, and the people who keep it open deserve thanks by name.
- **The ITU-T** publishes [H.264](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-H.264/en), H.265, [H.273](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-H.273/en) and H.274 as free downloads, and the ITU-R publishes [BT.2100](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.2100-3-202502-I/en).
- **ISO and IEC** list ISO/IEC 14496-12:2026, the ISO base media file format where the StereoVideoBox lives, at CHF 0 ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/113218)). I have my copy that way.
- **ETSI** publishes the DVB frame-compatible 3DTV specification, [TS 101 547-2](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/101500_101599/10154702/01.02.01_60/ts_10154702v010201p.pdf).
- **CIPA** publishes [DC-007 and DC-006](https://www.cipa.jp/e/std/std-sec.html), the multi-picture and stereo still image formats.
- **VESA** offers E-EDID and DisplayID through a download form.
- **The IETF** publishes RFC 9559 (Matroska), and the Matroska project publishes its element specification.
- **The Web3D Consortium, W3C and Khronos** publish theirs in the open.

A specification can be read by anyone and still be maintained by a body that works.

## Why the prices are what they are

Standards bodies explain that selling their texts pays for developing and maintaining them.
This page does not dispute that.
It only puts the cost next to the work that has to follow every clause, and next to the people who would do that work.
